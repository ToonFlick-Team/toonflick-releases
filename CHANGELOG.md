# Changelog

ToonFlick (engine: Bogo). Versions go up in larger steps; each lists what was added.

## 0.17.0 — Light, Bones and 3D (2026-11-13)

Light that takes a shape: gobos, built in or your own picture, lights that fall only on chosen layers, and a beam in the air you can turn off. Bones that bend, swing and fall: bendy bones, Spline IK, rubber-hose limbs, delayed bones, follow-through with weight, colliders. 3D models (GLB, glTF, OBJ) in a toon look as picture layers. Also merge drawing, frame scripts and bitmap fills as in Animate, falloff and simplify for points, grunge on gradients, number fields that look and drag the same everywhere, and new examples for all of it.

### Added
- Beam in the air (Properties ▸ Light): off takes a light's haze away on every frame (its keys too), so only the light and its gobo's pattern show where they land, a projection without a beam; on brings a light haze back. Scripts: light_layer.set beam.
- 3D models as picture layers: File ▸ Import ▸ 3D Model… (or Any File, or a file dropped on the window) brings a GLB, glTF 2.0 or OBJ model in as a picture layer, rendered on the GPU in a toon look (two to six tones, a tinted shadow, a rim light and an outline) or with its own textures smoothly lit. It can be a still at a moment of one of its animation clips, or animated: one picture per frame, looping or holding the last, at its own speed and from a frame you choose. Skeletons (skins) and keyed nodes play; morph targets, cameras and lights in the file are left out and said so in the import report. Layer ▸ 3D Model… opens the 3D window: drag to turn the model, Shift+drag to move it, the wheel to zoom, drag the sun to light it; view it as wireframe, solid, quick or final, pick its clip and moment, and Done renders the layer's pictures again. The pictures are a cache, rendered again only when the model file or a setting changes. Scripts: model.import, model.set, model.render, model.info, model.window. The model's files are read by the app's own readers, checked against limits (sizes, counts, depth, pictures only from beside the model).
- An example for 3D models, A 3D fox in the autumn street (Help ▸ Examples ▸ Production): the Khronos glTF sample fox walks along the autumn school's street on the street's plane of the multiplane stand, at the pace of its feet, then stops and looks round (a second layer on its Survey clip).
- Gobos: a light can shine through a pattern (Properties ▸ Light ▸ Gobo): window panes, blinds, bars, leaves or dots, with its size, turn, softness and shift, all keyable (key the shift to make leaves drift or blinds roll). With haze the patterned beam shows in the air. Scripts: light_layer.set gobo, gobo_size, gobo_turn, gobo_soft, gobo_shift.
- Your own picture as a gobo (Properties ▸ Light ▸ Gobo ▸ Picture…): a light shines through a PNG or JPG, the picture laid in the beam like glass (transparent and white let the light through, dark lets least through, coloured glass tints it like a stained-glass window), tiled and placed by the gobo's size, turn, softness and shift; a small preview shows it, Pick another… swaps it. The picture is kept beside the project like an image layer's, packaged with it and checked by the doctor; when it is missing the light shines plain. The picture is tiled, or with Repeat off it lies there once and the light around it shines as it is (one silhouette's shadow); a copy of it lies centred halfway along the light's reach: aim the light and set its reach to place it. Scripts: light_layer.set gobo "picture", gobo_picture, gobo_repeat.
- An example for the picture gobo (Help ▸ Examples ▸ Light & effects ▸ Stained glass in the chapel): the afternoon sun through a stained-glass window out of the shot lays the rose, the dove and the ribbons in colour on a village chapel's flagstones, the pews and the altar staying in the shade; the sun moves on, the colours slide and warm. The pictures were generated by AI.
- Lights that light chosen layers (a projector): Properties ▸ Light ▸ Lights only picks the layers a spot or point light falls on, its receivers; only they catch its light and its gobo's pattern, and whatever is drawn in front of them stays unlit, while its haze in the air and its lens flare stay on the whole picture. A receiver's surface can be folded (Properties ▸ Light surface ▸ Folded, with where the fold line is, its angle and the bend), as a floor meeting a back wall: the pattern lands on the surface unfolded, so it breaks at the corner and is foreshortened on the floor. A receiver tilted in 3D (Rotation X/Y) catches it on its tilted plane. Scripts: light_layer.set receivers, layer.light_fold. Examples: Evening sun in an empty attic (the window panes break at the corner of the wall and the floor) and A walk under the trees (dappled light on a walker in a close-up).
- Five examples for gobos (Help ▸ Examples ▸ Light & effects), one for each pattern: moonlight through an attic studio's window on the floor only (Window), sunset through an office's blinds on the floor and the back wall, breaking at the corner, the blinds rolling shut (Blinds), a pierced lantern's turning dots in a tea room (Dots), dappled sun swaying over a forest picnic (Leaves) and the sinking sun sweeping jail bars across a sheriff's office (Bars). The pictures were generated by AI.
- Follow-through bones can be whole and have a weight: Whole bone makes the bone hang and swing from where the rig puts it (its start moves too, the bones under it go along) instead of only turning, and Weight makes it heavier (it swings slower and hangs lower). Collides keeps it out of collider bones and collider shapes. A whole bone with no parent falls freely (faster and faster, without turning in the air) until it lands on a collider, where it rubs to a stop and can tip over; Falls at most stops it that far below where the rig puts it. Scripts: bone.spring whole, weight, collide, fall.
- Collider shapes: any closed shape can be a collider (Properties ▸ Collider): follow-through bones that collide and wobbling parts with Collide on can't go into it. It can be animated; the simulation meets it between frames too (sub-steps). Scripts: shape.set_style collider.
- An example for bone physics, Lanterns in the wind (Help ▸ Examples ▸ Characters & rigs): lanterns of different weights swing in gusts of wind, the same lantern on a chain (it only turns) and on a spring (a whole bone), and a cart of apples whose collider pushes the ribbons aside. The pictures were generated by AI.
- An example for bones that fall, Falling fruit (Help ▸ Examples ▸ Characters & rigs): in an autumn orchard apples and a pear drop from the tree one by one and land on the bench, a leaning plank (and slide down it), a crate and the grass (invisible colliders), and a spider swings on its thread, a chain of follow-through bones that bends, as the twig sways each time. The pictures were generated by AI.
- Grunge on gradients: in the gradient editor, Grunge (0–1) makes the colours spatter into each other instead of changing smoothly, as if sprayed, and Speck sets how big a speck is; on fills and lines, in renders too. Scripts: shape.gradient grunge, grunge_size.
- Grain size for the Noise effect: 1 px is the grain as before, bigger makes bigger, soft grains (film grain, paper). Scripts: the noise effect's value `size`.
- A brush can take the fill along: in the Brushes window, "The fill's edge moves too" makes the fill's edge wobble with the line's roughness and boil with it, so the fill stays under the line (shape.set_style and style.set: fill_moves).
- Delayed bones: a bone turns as the bone above it did some frames before, so a motion runs down a chain like a wave without keys (a tail, antennae, a feather, an ear). Two ways: by angle only, or with position too, when it also trails behind where it was (a hat, a loose part). Bone ▸ Control Rig ▸ Make Delayed Chain, or Properties ▸ Rig ▸ Delay (frames, how much, Position). Scripts: `rig.delay_chain`, `bone.rig` (`delay`, `delay_amount`, `delay_position`).
- Four examples for the new bones, each on a plain stage with plain bones beside the new kind on the same keys (Help ▸ Examples ▸ Characters & rigs): Bamboo (a plain bone and a bendy one that IK bends like a pole), Teapot dance (plain and rubber-hose limbs, a body that keeps its volume, an arm switched IK → FK and back), Tails (a tail swung from its root and one on spline IK) and Delayed bones (plain, by angle, with position). The gallery's New in 0.17.0 lists only this version's examples.
- Spline IK: a chain (a tail, a spine, a rope, hair) lies along a smooth curve through a few handles, so you pose and animate the handles, not every bone. Bone ▸ Control Rig ▸ Add Spline IK makes the handles (3 by default) along the chain as it is drawn, riding with the bone above it; the curve shows dashed on the stage. Properties ▸ Bone: Stretch (the chain stretches to the curve's length, otherwise it keeps its length), Follows (keyed, 0–100 %) and Remove. Scripts: `rig.spline_ik`, `rig.spline_ik_set`.
- Rubber-hose limbs: Bone ▸ Control Rig ▸ Make Rubber-Hose Limb turns one bone into the classic cartoon arm or leg: bendy, keeping its length, bowing just enough to reach the IK controller at its tip (and straight, pointing, when the controller is further). Scripts: `rig.rubber_hose`.
- Switch IK ↔ FK (Bone ▸ Control Rig) on any frame, keeping the pose: to FK the limb's bones are keyed where IK had them, to IK its controller is keyed on the tip; the switch holds until the next one. Scripts: `rig.ik_fk`.
- Keeps its volume (Properties ▸ Bone): a stretched bone gets as much thinner, a squashed one fatter, so what is bound to it keeps its volume. Scripts: `bone.set` (`keep_volume`).
- The pose library applies a pose whole, mirrored (left and right swapped), on the selected bones only (a hand, the face) or halfway from the pose now (Bone ▸ Pose ▸ Pose Library ▸ Apply ▸ …); poses keep a bendy bone's bend. Scripts: `pose.apply` (`mirror`, `only_selected`, `amount`).
- Bendy bones: a bone split into segments bends along a curve, and what is bound to it (points, layers bound whole, flexi-binding) follows the curve, so one bone bends an arm, a tail or a neck smoothly. Properties ▸ Bone: Bendy (segments), Bend (keyed, in degrees) and Ease in and out (how long the bone keeps its direction at either end); a bent bone is drawn along its curve on the stage. The setup frame's bend is how the artwork was drawn; children stay on the bone's end. A selected bendy bone has a handle in its middle (Transform Bones and Pose Bones): drag it across the bone to bend it, along it to move the bend towards the start or the end (Ctrl: only bend); posing keys it on the frame. Keeps its length: a bent bone's ends come closer, as a bent stick's do. IK bends it: in an IK chain the bone bends to reach instead of only turning, like a pole (its start keeps its direction, as bamboo or a fishing rod pulled at the tip) or like a hose (it keeps its length and bows to reach). An even ease bends the bone in a true circular arc. Scripts: `bone.set` (`bend`, `segments`, `ease_in`, `ease_out`, `keep_length`, `ik_bend`: none, pole or hose; bending a straight bone makes it bendy with 8 segments).Older files open with straight bones.
- Rotation X and Rotation Y on top-level layers: tilt a plane about its pivot, and the camera sees it in true perspective, as a card turning away or a floor or wall laid into the scene (Rotation X 90° lays a layer down as a floor). Keyed like the other transform channels, in Properties ▸ Transform, on the timeline and in the Graph; depth order and parallax stay as they were, and what reaches behind the camera is cut off. The 3D View shows the planes tilted, and its Rotate gizmo (R) has a red and a green ring for X and Y next to the blue one, keyed on the current frame. Scripts: `layer.set` (`rotation_x`, `rotation_y`), `view3d.transform` (`rotate_x`, `rotate_y`). Files without them open flat as before.
- Points on a tilted layer are edited where you see them: a dragged point or handle follows the pointer on the tilted plane, a new line or shape is drawn on the plane where you draw it, and double-clicking a curve adds a point there.
- Duplicate to New Layer (Ctrl+Alt+D, Edit menu and the right-click menu of selected shapes): the selected shapes are copied into a new vector layer right above, exactly where they are.
- Falloff for moving points (Points tool options): the points around the ones you drag come along, less the further they are. Reach (Shift+wheel on the stage, also while dragging), a curve (Smooth, Sphere, Root, Sharp, Linear, Inverse square, Constant) and Only connected: only the same shape, by the distance along its outline, so the other lip stays put. The reach shows round the point; the points it takes are ringed by how much. points.falloff sets it; points.move takes falloff.
- Simplify in the Points tool's bar (and its right-click menu): fewer points, the same curve, within a tolerance (± px). It takes out only the points the curve can do without — the selected points, or all of the selected shapes' — and the points left are the same points, with their keys, bones and line widths; a Bézier curve's handles stretch to make up. Draw ▸ Path ▸ Simplify… does the same (other curve types as before).
- Turn one shape into another (Draw ▸ Path ▸ Turn Into the Other Shape): of two selected shapes, the lower one becomes the upper one between two frames, even when they don't match point for point (a circle into a star, a cloud into a rabbit). Both get as many points along their outlines, lined up and the same way round; a hole the other lacks grows from, or shrinks to, a point. Scripts: `shape.morph`.
- Shape tweens from Adobe Animate files (FLA and XFL) that don't match point for point are now animated with the same matching, instead of jumping from keyframe to keyframe.
- Merge drawing (Draw ▸ Merge Drawing, and in the Draw tool's right-click menu), as in Animate: a filled shape you draw cuts the shapes under it on the same layer, and joins those of the same fill colour into one. Off by default. Scripts: `draw.merge_drawing`, or `merge` on `shape.add`, `shape.rect` and `shape.ellipse`.
- Frame scripts: playback stops on a frame, or goes on from another one (a loop), as `stop()` and `gotoAndPlay()` do in Animate (Animation ▸ Markers ▸ Stop Playback Here; an orange mark on the ruler). FLA and XFL imports keep their frame scripts: `stop()`, `play()`, `gotoAndPlay()` and `gotoAndStop()` (to a frame or a label) run, and the rest is kept to read. Scripts: `anim.frame_action`.
- Fill with a picture (Draw ▸ Fill With Picture…), a bitmap fill as in Animate: closed shapes are filled with a picture instead of a colour, which covers the shape and moves, turns and stretches like a gradient. Scripts: `shape.picture_fill`.
- A Flatpak of the Linux build (`scripts/package-flatpak.sh`, from the release tarball), beside the .deb and the AppImage.
- Fill and line as a pair under the drawing tools, as in other drawing apps: the fill a square in front, the line a frame behind it. A click brings one to the front, and the palette and recent colours set the one in front (Shift+click the other); a click on the one in front opens its colour picker. Beside it: swap (X), white and black (D) and none (no fill or no line on new shapes).
- An accessibility page (`docs/accessibility.md`): what ToonFlick does for people who see, move or hear differently, and what isn't there yet.
### Changed
- Every number you can change looks the same, a dark field with a thin edge (lighter under the pointer, blue while you drag or type): the blue numbers in Properties and Rig, the boxes in Scene, Palette, Export and the tool bars, the numbers beside sliders and the timeline's timecode. Drag it, click to type, a comma works as the decimal point everywhere. Numbers you can't change are plain grey text. Sliders that hid their number (Taper, Roughness, Stabiliser, Tween) show it, sizes and widths say px, and Boil is the same slider (0–240 frames) in the tool bar and the Brushes window.
- Properties ▸ Rig: Collider and Follow-Through are in a section of their own, Physics.
- New bones keep their volume (Properties ▸ Bone ▸ Keeps its volume is on): stretched, what is bound to them gets thinner. Older files and imported rigs stay as they were.
- In a split 3D View each view's switch (Active Camera, Top, …) sits over its own view instead of both over the left one.
- Icons in the strip beside Properties: Layer is a page, Camera a film camera (also the Camera tool), and the two effects are told apart by a letter: L for the layer's, S for the scene's. The strip goes from what is selected to the whole shot: Layer, Compositing, Rig, the layer's effects, Review, the scene's effects, Camera, Scene.
- Rig mode no longer throws you into another mode when a click in the window picks a tool of another mode (for example a click on a drawing in Rig that would pick points): the Rig tool stays and the status says how to leave (Alt+number or Tab). Commands and MCP still switch as before.
- Icons everyone knows: the app now uses the common icons from Lucide (ISC licence) where other animation and graphics apps use them. Select is the arrow cursor, Transform a box with corners, Freehand a pencil, Pen the pen nib, Eraser, Blur (a drop), Fill (a paint bucket), Eyedropper (a pipette), Ink Bottle (a flask), Rectangle region (dashed square), Pan (an open hand), Zoom (a magnifier), Rotate and Reset rotation (arrows), Camera, Previous and Next frame (step arrows), Onion skin (stacked layers), Motion paths and Vector layer (a curve), Raster layer and Weight paint (a brush), Image layer, Switch layer, Mesh, Release and Reset pose, the three 3D gizmos, the keyframe diamond, Note and Blend. Bone tools are a bone with a badge: plus (add), arrows (move) and T (transform). The Gradient icon stays.
- Symmetry at hand: in the Points tool's right-click menu (Mirror editing, the axis through the selection or the middle, a level axis, Symmetrize, Mirror shapes, Remove axis) and in the quick settings (right button held, left click): Mirror editing, Symmetrize, Axis.
- Properties ▸ Transform is laid out as in 3D editors: Position X Y Z (Z is the depth), Rotation X Y Z and Scale X Y, each row with one key for all its axes. Z and Rotation X and Y are there for top-level layers, which the camera sees in depth.
- The right-click menu of selected shapes has Paste, Duplicate and Duplicate to New Layer next to Copy.
- Properties: a drawing's rig has its own page, Rig, on the strip: bone binding (Flexi-binding), Morphs & correctives and Wobble. The Drawing · Compositing page keeps the shapes, symmetry, particles and compositing.
- Solo (S in Layers) has two modes: the first click keeps the other layers at half strength and only the solo layer (and what's in it) can be picked; the second shows only the solo layer; the third turns it off. The layer's right-click menu has both modes and Solo Off; layer.solo takes mode fade, only or off.
- The Effect Guide looks like the Examples window: groups on the left, cards with a colour bar and what each does, and the chosen effect big on the right with Add at the bottom.

### Fixed
- Numbers in Properties ▸ Light and Light surface, a layer's and a shape's size, points' position, particles, text and its animation, the reference, a fill's feather, a morph's weight and the timeline's end and fps could hardly be dragged (they only took the last bit of the drag when let go); they change as you drag now.
- The numbers in Properties ▸ Rig (Follow-Through, Limits, Delay, Collider and the rest) could not be dragged: they sprang back as you dragged. They change as you drag now, and a drag is one Undo.
- Blend modes (Multiply and the others) show on the stage with onion skin, Fade Other Layers or Solo with the others faded on: the current layer, drawn again on top of the rest, was drawn as Normal (and with its opacity and effects lost, a see-through layer twice). It's now drawn once, on top, with its blend, opacity and effects, on pictures, vector layers and groups alike; renders were right all along.
- The move gizmo's z arm in the 3D views moves only the depth: Keep size no longer scales and moves the plane with it (only dragging the plane itself keeps its look).
- A bent bendy bone is picked on its curve (before, only on the straight line between its ends, so a bent one couldn't be clicked); box selection takes it by the middle of its curve.
- Keeps its volume is now right under Stretch in Properties ▸ Bone, for every bone: stretched, what is bound to it gets thinner (it is off by default, when a stretch only makes it longer).
- Dragging a number with the mouse (any value in the panels, and the like) is one step in History, undone to where it was before the press; it used to be a step a frame, so Undo was of no use.
- A small shape inside a bigger one is picked with one click, even when the bigger one covers it (Alt+click goes on to the bigger one). A click on a shape that isn't selected picks the whole shape first, then its points; before, a click near one of the edited layer's points took the point, and a shape of the edited layer won over the one on top.
- Points: picking one point of a shape keeps the shape's other points on show, so the next one can be picked too; only the points of the shapes you edit show (editing the mouth, the head's points stay hidden). All Points (View ▸ Show) still shows every point of the layer.
- Solo works again: the stage didn't redraw when a layer was soloed.
- The Effect Guide no longer moves under the pointer: the column with the chosen effect is always there (the first one shows at the start), so a second click lands on the card you aimed at.
- Render, Save As and export offered the project's name as the file name with a colon in it ("Busker: a flat rig"), which Windows refuses, so the file didn't save in one click; the colon now becomes a dash, and the other characters Windows refuses an underscore.

## 0.16.0 — Depth and the 3D View (2026-11-08)

A scene with depth: layers on a multiplane camera, a 3D View to see and arrange them, with a gizmo, and a Compose workspace for it. Also onion skin per side with Shift & Trace, symmetry and line clean-up, imports from Adobe Animate and Flash, export to Lottie, the Breakdown Assistant, the Paint Bucket through all layers, a new start window, and tutorials for all of it.

### Added
- Onion skin, each side its own: Animation ▸ Onion Skin sets a tint and an opacity for the drawings before and another for the drawings after.
- Onion skin ▸ Snap to Keys (magnetic ghosts, after Moho): a ghost counted in frames moves onto a keyframe within half a step of it, so it shows a pose rather than an in-between. It never crosses the current frame.
- Onion skin ▸ Shift & Trace, as on a light table (after Harmony): move, turn and size the ghosts before and the ghosts after apart from the stage, to draw the in-between where they overlap. The drawings themselves don't move; turning Shift & Trace off shows the ghosts in place again, and Reset Shift & Trace puts them back. Scripts: `anim.onion_shift`.
- Symmetry on a vector layer: Draw ▸ Symmetry ▸ Set Axis, or Properties ▸ Symmetry. The axis is drawn across the stage. With Mirror editing on, moving a point or a handle moves its partner in the mirror, a point on the axis stays on it, new lines and shapes are drawn mirrored, a point added on a line is added on its mirrored line too, and deleting a point deletes its partner.
- Symmetrize (Draw ▸ Symmetry, or Properties): select points that are only nearly symmetric and they become exact mirrors, handles too; points close to the axis go onto it. Either both sides meet halfway, or the left or the right one stays and the other is mirrored from it.
- Mirror Shapes makes a mirrored copy of the selected shapes across the axis.
- Properties ▸ Transform with points or shapes selected shows the selection: the middle of its box, its size, a scale and a turn. Typing or dragging a number moves, sizes, scales or turns just the selection, along the layer's own axes; the Scale lock keeps its proportions. With nothing selected it is the layer's again.
- Hide a line in a vector drawing without moving it to a layer of its own: Hide in Properties, or Draw ▸ Hide Lines. It leaves the stage and the render and can't be picked, but stays in the drawing. Properties lists the hidden lines, each with Show, and Show all brings them back.
- Hide one segment of a line, the stretch between two points next to each other: select both and Draw ▸ Points ▸ Hide Segment, or Segment ▸ Hide in Properties. The fill and the points stay; Show Segment draws it again.
- Join lines at two selected end points: select an end of each line and Draw ▸ Path ▸ Join Lines, or Segment ▸ Join in Properties; both ends of one line close it. With whole lines selected it still joins their closest ends.
- Draw ▸ Path ▸ Trim Tails cuts the ends of lines drawn past their last crossing, so a sketchy outline comes out clean and ready to fill. Only short ends are cut (by default up to 8 % of the drawing's size); a long free line such as a fold stays. It works on the selected lines, or on all of the layer's. A cut end tapers off over twice the line's width, and is welded onto the line it crossed: that line gets a point there, or uses one already there, and the end sits on it.
- File ▸ Import ▸ Adobe Animate (FLA, XFL)… brings an Animate document (CS5 or later: an .fla, an XFL folder or its .xfl file) in as a new project. Layers, folders and masks come as layers, groups and masking groups; guide layers come hidden. A layer whose drawing changes becomes a switch layer stepping on its keyframes. Classic tweens become transform and opacity keys with Animate's ease, extra turns included, and shape tweens become point keys where the two drawings match point for point. Each symbol comes once, into a hidden "Animate library" group, and every instance is a reference layer to it on its own clock: a graphic loops, plays once or holds a frame as set. Pictures and sounds are saved in a folder beside the document; frame labels become markers, and several scenes play one after another. The import report lists what didn't come over or came only nearly: ActionScript, filters, colour effects other than alpha, skew, motion guides, Animate's newer motion tweens (shown as their keyframes), video and the like. Built on documents we wrote ourselves: tell us how your files come in.
- Points and strokes that help the tracer with lines: in the Trace window, pick the point tool and tap where a line belongs — a gap closes there, or a faint stretch stays — or Alt-tap where none does, so lines that touch part there. Hold and drag to draw a stroke instead: the line goes along it, or with Alt nothing does. Drag a point or stroke to move it, Ctrl-click to take it away.
- Brightness and Contrast in the Trace window change the picture before it's traced, so a faint pencil line comes up; the picture shows the change. Auto sets both from the picture: the paper white, the lines dark. In the Over view a slider sets how much of the original shows under the result.
- The Trace window's Points tool shows the result's points: drag one where it belongs and it stays there when you change the settings and trace again, the fill's edge following it. Ctrl-click a moved (blue) point gives it back to the tracer; Clear Points takes them all away.
- Presets in the Trace window: Clean line art, Model sheet, Line and colour and Flat colour picture set the look and the numbers in one pick; changing a number shows "Your own".
- Example: A sheet of hands (clean line art), sixty inked hands traced with the Clean line art preset.
- Onion skin on pictures and raster drawings: the ghosts of drawings before and after are tinted like vector ones, strongest on dark lines and faint on white paper.
- Onion skin counts drawings and panels (Animation ▸ Onion Skin ▸ Count Drawings and Panels, on by default). On a cel or switch layer the ghosts are the drawings before and after, from anywhere on a hold, and only that layer is ghosted. In a storyboard they are the panel before (as it ends) and the panel after (as it starts).
- A moodboard: reference pictures pinned beside the work, in the Moodboard panel (Window ▸ Panels, and a tab in Storyboard mode). Add pictures or a whole folder, drop or paste them on it; move, size, note and tidy them; zoom and pan the board. Right-click a picture to put it on the stage as a locked, faint guide. The pictures go with the project when it's packaged, and are never rendered. The example The dog in the rain (a storyboard) has one.
- Frames from a video: File ▸ Import ▸ Frames from a Video…, or drop a clip on the window. Look through it, pick a range (every Nth frame) or frames one by one, and bring them in as a column on the timeline, pictures on the moodboard, a Trace Sequence, or only files. Uses the ffmpeg that comes with the app.
- Shortcuts you change: in Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts click a command's or a tool's keys and press new ones. A key already in use is named and can be taken over. Your keys are kept in the preferences and the cheat sheet shows them; Help ▸ Default Shortcuts puts them all back.
- A scene with depth: a top-level layer can sit behind the picture (Camera ▸ Depth ▸ Layer Depth…, or Depth in Properties), keeping its look on the current frame. A camera pan then moves far layers less than near ones, and Dolly In… moves the camera into the scene, near layers growing faster and passing it, as on a multiplane stand. Spread Layers in Depth… places them all at once; Camera Distance… sets the lens.
- Example: Luna in depth (a multiplane camera), the Luna street in planes, the camera trucking along it and pulling back.
- Examples painted for the multiplane camera, in the New list of Examples: An autumn school in depth (six painted planes, maple leaves and a mint van before a brick school), A sunny room in depth (six painted planes, the sun in the window with a lens flare and dust in the light as particles) and A cartoon room in depth (the same room drawn flat, on the same planes).
- 3D View (Camera ▸ Depth ▸ 3D View, or Window ▸ Panels), after After Effects' 3D views: the depth scene with its top-level layers as planes at their depths, the camera with its field of view and its path. Two panes side by side, each Active Camera, Top, Left, Right, Front or a Custom view, both live while playing. Drag to orbit, Shift+drag or the middle button to pan, the wheel to dolly; double-click or Frame puts the view back. These move only the view, never the scene camera: orbiting the Active Camera opens the Custom view from there. In Top, Left and Right drag a plane to change its depth (Keep size keeps its look through the camera, as Layer Depth… does) or a diamond on the camera's path to move the camera's key there. Persp / Ortho shows a view in perspective or flat, looking the same way at the same place (the Active Camera is always in perspective: it is what renders). Scripts: `view3d.show`, `view3d.navigate`, `camera.path_point`.

## 0.13.0 — Graph editor & motion paths (2026-10-24)
- **Curves like a studio graph editor**: every key can have a **tangent mode** — Auto (smooth, never past the keys around it), Spline (smooth through the keys), Linear, Flat or Free — with **handles that have a weight** (how far they pull) and can be **broken** (in and out apart) or unified; keys without one keep their interpolation and ease. A channel's **extrapolation** says what happens before its first key and after its last: hold, carry on at the same speed, loop, loop with an offset (walks that go on) or ping-pong. Old projects open unchanged. Commands: `curve.tangent`, `curve.extrapolate`; `anim.graph` reports tangents and extrapolation.
- **Curve tools** (Animation ▸ Curves, the Graph's Tools): **Simplify** (fewer keys, the same motion within a tolerance), **Smooth Keys**, **Bake to Every Frame**, **Euler Filter** (rotations without whole-turn jumps) and **Tween** (a breakdown key between the keys around the playhead, at any fraction — beyond them overshoots). Commands: `curve.simplify`, `curve.smooth`, `curve.bake`, `curve.euler`, `curve.tween`.
- **A new Graph editor**: a **channel tree** grouped by layer, bone or shape, with search, and an eye, solo and lock on every curve (click: pick its keys, double-click: frame it); a **frame ruler** (drag to scrub, markers, the play range and the shot's end shaded) and a **value axis**, zoomed with the wheel (Ctrl: values, Shift: pan) and panned with the middle button or Alt+drag; **F** frames everything, **Shift+F** the selected keys; curves in their axis colours, **dashed where they extrapolate**, with **ghosts** of how they were while you edit; four views — **Values**, **Normalized** (every curve from −1 to 1), **Stacked** (each in its own lane) and **Speed** (how fast a channel moves, to even out motion). **Keys** are picked by click, Shift / Ctrl and box, **moved in time** (whole frames) **and value** (Shift locks an axis), and shown by their tangent mode; **a box round several keys scales** their timing (its sides) or amplitude (top and bottom); the selected keys' **tangent handles** pull slope and weight, Alt breaks them; **Ctrl+click on a curve adds a key**, I keys every curve on the playhead, Delete, Ctrl+A / C / V, right-click: invert values, pick the keys on the playhead; the **numbers below** edit frame, value, slopes and weights exactly (arithmetic and +10 work). The bar has the tangent modes, Step, Break / Unify, **eases** (in, out, both, strong, back, overshoot, bounce, elastic), **extrapolation before and after**, a **tween slider**, the tools (also reverse in time, anticipation, overshoot and settle, bounce), value snapping and **pinning** the graph to its curves. The keys picked in the timeline and in the graph follow each other.
- **Motion paths you edit on the stage** (Animation ▸ Motion Paths ▸ Show): **drag a key** to move it where it should be — a layer or an IK controller moves its Position, a bone turns to reach (parents, IK and constraints are followed); **Alt+drag** moves the key **in time** along the path (let go on the frame that should reach that place); a picked key shows its **handles** — pull them to **bend the path** (both turn together until broken); **Ctrl+click** the path adds a key on that frame; double-click goes to the key's frame; the dots between keys show the spacing. **Orient Along Path** (Animation ▸ Motion Paths) turns a layer or bone to face where it is going. Keys picked on the path, in the graph and in the timeline follow each other. Commands: `path.move_key`, `path.handle`, `path.add_key`, `path.retime_key`, `path.orient`.
- **Example: Graph editor & paths** (Motion): a ball whose bounce is three keys shaped by tangents — level at the top, broken at the floor — and hops on by extrapolation, and a bee on a path bent by a handle, turned along it.

## 0.11.0 — Physics, particles & interchange (2026-10-23)
- **Shape-keeping physics**: wobbling layers can keep their shape — *stretch* holds neighbouring points (or mesh edges) at the distance the rig gives them, *bend* keeps their curve — so ropes, flags, hair and cloth swing without stretching like rubber. Solved in fixed sub-steps, deterministic, bakeable.
- **Collider bones**: any bone can be a collider (Properties ▸ Control rig ▸ Collider radius): a capsule along it that wobbling parts can't enter — a ball as a short bone, a thin segment with radius 0.
- **Gravity & wind** (Scene ▸ Gravity & wind): one gravity and a wind with a keyable strength, gusts and turbulence that push wobble and follow-through bones; each layer chooses how much it feels them. Example: File ▸ Examples ▸ Motion ▸ Flag in the wind.
- **Particles** (Layer ▸ Particles): any layer can emit particles — its drawing (or picture) is drawn once per particle — born in a point, line, rectangle or circle, with rate and bursts, life, speed, direction and spread, gravity, the scene's wind, drag, spin, facing where they fly, size and fading over their life, bouncing off collider bones and a ground line (or dying there), and a warm-up so snow is everywhere from the first frame. Ready presets: rain, snow, sparks and smoke. Deterministic: stage, export and the command line show the same particles. Example: File ▸ Examples ▸ Light & effects ▸ Campfire in the snow.
- Pictures can fade (particles made of images fade out).
- **Clean lines under squash and stretch**: squashed and stretched drawings keep an even line all round; a layer or group can *keep its line width* whatever it is scaled by (Properties ▸ Lines), so a character keeps the same line weight close up and far away.
- **OpenEXR sequences** (Render ▸ Export ▸ OpenEXR sequence, `render.final` format exr): half-float, linear light, premultiplied alpha — for compositing. Written without extra libraries.
- **Audio turnover** (File ▸ Export ▸ Audio Turnover): the shot's sound from its first to its last frame plus handles, as 24-bit WAV with the source's channels and rate.
- **OpenTimelineIO round-trip** (File ▸ Export / Import ▸ OpenTimelineIO): a folder of shots as clips on an editorial timeline (cut, handles, render, project); trims and extensions made in the edit come back as the shots' in/out frames.

## 0.10.0 — Motion, light & review (2026-10-23)

## 0.8.0 — Meshes, studio foundation & look (2026-10-22)
- Deformation meshes (0.8 groundwork): `mesh.create` builds a triangle mesh under a vector layer's closed, filled shapes (constrained Delaunay with refinement, holes, extra vertices at joints; open lines reported); `skin.auto_bind` skins it to the skeleton (up to 4 bones per vertex, normalised, explicit bind pose); fills and outlines ride the mesh without cracks. `mesh.inspect` lists problems with stable codes; rebuilding a mesh keeps its weights. Project format 2 (format 1 files upgrade on load).
- Weight Paint tool (G): paint / add / subtract / smooth a bone's weights with a heat map and the mesh wireframe; Normalize, Mirror L→R / R→L (left/right bones paired by name), Create/Rebuild mesh and Auto bind in its bar. Commands `skin.paint`, `skin.set_weights`, `skin.mirror`.
- Mesh editing: add a vertex (click in Weight Paint ▸ Edit mesh, or `mesh.add_vertex`), split an edge, remove a vertex (Alt-click) — the mesh stays valid, existing vertices keep their IDs and new ones blend their neighbours' weights. Rebind (`skin.rebind`) takes the skeleton's new setup pose without losing painted weights; its preview says how far the drawing would otherwise be off.
- Rendering: groups, blend modes and effects use about 1–2 ms less GPU time per frame than the first blend-mode build; offscreen buffers a scene no longer needs are released after a few seconds; `app.stats` reports geometry reuse, offscreen buffers and their memory.
- Reproducible output: golden-frame tests (rigid, flexi, skinned, blended, effects) and migration fixtures guard the look and the file format; `scripts/qa-0.8.sh` runs the whole headless check, `docs/0.8/QA.md` the manual one. `bogo-cli bench --meshes N --effects` adds skinned-mesh and effect stress.
- Deformation debugger: the Project Doctor poses every skinned mesh on the setup, every bone key and a sample of the animation, and reports where the drawing folds over (`deform.folded`) or stretches more than ×3 (`deform.stretch`); `mesh.debug` gives the per-frame numbers. Mesh quality profiles (draft / standard / fine) and `skin.copy_weights` from another mesh of the same skeleton.
- Project Doctor (File ▸ Project Doctor…, `bogo-cli validate`, MCP `project.doctor`): missing files, broken rig references, invalid meshes and weights, effect settings, shot ranges and duplicate IDs, each with a severity, a stable code, where it is and a suggested fix. CLI exit code 2 on errors.
- Portable packages: `bogo-cli package` copies a project and its files into a folder with relative paths and a manifest (sizes, SHA-256); `bogo-cli verify` finds missing or damaged files and refuses paths that leave the package. `bogo-cli inspect` prints a JSON summary.
- Shot identity: show, sequence, shot, frame in/out and handles (`project.shot`).
- Drawings on a mesh bend along it: their curves are resampled along the mesh, so straight edges follow a bent joint.
- 360° rigs: view dials (turn a character or a head through front, left, back and right), a setup pose per view for every bone so one animation works from every side, switch layers driven by a dial.
- Smooth turns: a dial with "smooth turn" blends the bone setups between two views and morphs the drawings of neighbouring views when they are built alike (same layers, shapes and points).
- Stage selection follows the usual rules in every tool: a click picks what's on top under the pointer (any layer) and a press on it drags it; Shift adds, Ctrl toggles, Alt takes away from a box; clicking empty space or Esc deselects; what a click would pick is highlighted. Transform Layer selects whole groups (double-click enters a group, Ctrl+click picks the part) and box-selects layers; bone tools pick bones of any character.
- Region selection as in ToonFlick: rectangle, ellipse or lasso (option bar of Select Points and Transform Layer), dashed outline; Shift adds, Ctrl flips what's inside, Alt takes away; Select all / Select none / Delete in the bar.
- Layer outlines: any layer — above all a group — can have an outline around its whole silhouette (colour, width), drawn on the GPU from the composited group, so shapes inside it merge into one outlined figure.
- Blend modes: a layer or group can mix with what's below it as multiply, screen, overlay, add, darken, lighten, colour dodge or colour burn (Properties ▸ Blend, `layer.set blend`), on the GPU with the standard formulas.
- Effect stack: each layer has an ordered list of effects (outline, tint) with keyable settings — add, reorder, duplicate, switch off or remove them in Properties or with `effect.*` commands. Older files' layer outlines load as the first effect.
- Erase layers: a layer can cut itself out of what's below it in its group (build a figure by adding and subtracting parts; the group outline follows the holes).
- Moho import (File → Import Moho…, `bogo-cli moho`, MCP `file.import_moho`): bone, group, switch, vector and image layers with transforms and masking; skeletons with animation, IK targets and limits; vector art with bone-bound points, curvature, handle weights and angles, per-point widths, hidden segments, fills, outlines and named styles; compound shapes with holes; the camera; and Smart Bone actions — point moves become correctives, bone and layer moves become driven channels, drawing switches follow their bone.
- Compound shapes: one shape can hold several outlines (holes and islands, filled even-odd).
- Bones and layers can be driven by other bones' rotations (piecewise, per direction).
- Switches can be picked by a bone's rotation.
- 3D pose offsets in turning rigs: a bone that follows a body's view dial (hands, feet, poles) gets a depth channel, so its pose turns with the character — a hand reaching forward in the left view reaches forward in the right view, on the other side of the root. Dragging it moves it exactly as seen in the current view.
- Two-axis views: a dial can cover an arc instead of a full circle (a head's up/down tilt), and a second dial turns switches and bone setups into a grid of views (turn × tilt) that blends in both directions.
- Character Controls slide out from the right of the stage (fold them to a thin bar): a drawn face to grab and turn, pupils to drag for the look, eyebrows to raise and tilt; a figure to turn around with Front/Left/Back/Right stops; mouth and hand charts with real thumbnails of the drawings.
- "The Irishman", a 360° demo character: eight views drawn from one simple 3D model, so he turns around smoothly; his head also nods up and down (8 turns × 3 tilts).
