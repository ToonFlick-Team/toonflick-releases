# Changelog

ToonFlick (engine: Bogo). Versions go up in larger steps; each lists what was added.

## 0.14.1 — Fixes (2026-10-28)

### Fixed
- **Play is heard at once**: pressing Play on the setup frame (where a project opens), or before a sound clip starts, played the animation silently until it looped — the sound only came after scrubbing the timeline first. Now it comes in exactly when the playhead reaches it, in the render preview too; the sound output is also opened as soon as a project has sound, so the first Play doesn't miss its start.
- **The macOS app opens again**: in 0.14.0 Rhubarb's speech models sat among the app's code (Contents/MacOS), the signature check failed and macOS wouldn't open ToonFlick even with Open Anyway. They're in Contents/Resources now, and every macOS build checks its signature before it's packaged.
- The build server cross-builds bogo.exe for Windows with its icon and version details.

## 0.14.0 — Voice and performance (2026-10-28)

Characters that talk and act: dialogue generated with ElevenLabs or recorded, lip-synced offline with Rhubarb (it comes with ToonFlick), a timeline row for each speaker, mouths that bend instead of swap, joysticks recorded live, expressions on any value, light layers, Photoshop and SVG imported with their layers, and eight new tutorials.

### New
- **Eight new tutorials**: importing artwork (C5), music from MIDI (D7), expressions (D8), recording a joystick live (E10), a voice clip and lip-sync (F5), a mouth that bends (F6), light layers and lens flares (G4) and text that moves (G5).
- **Dialogue in the asset library**: a take of dialogue — its sound and its lines, letter by letter — published as a new asset kind (asset.publish kind dialogue, clip). Linked into another shot (asset.link, start) it lands as a sound clip on that frame and finds each speaker's mouth by the character's name (or the mouths you give), keyed at once — no new ElevenLabs call.
- **A row for each speaker** on the timeline, under the soundtrack: who speaks in the sound clips (the character whose mouth a line moves, or the voice), with their lines as bars (what is said on hover) and their mouth shapes to edit. Drag a line to move its clip in time — the mouths go with it and nothing is left where it was.
- **Record live**: in Pose Bones turn on the record button (●) and drag a joystick, slider or dial handle — the timeline plays from the current frame and every frame is keyed as you move (keys skipped by playback are cleared, the take ends when you let go or the timeline wraps), then thinned to the keys the motion needs. Performance animation with the mouse or a pen.
- **Expressions**: a formula on any animated value, worked out on every frame on top of its keys — Graph ▸ Motion ▾ ▸ Expression (or anim.modifier expr): `value` (what the keys give), `t` seconds, `frame`, `fps`, `part`; + − × ÷ % ^ and sin, cos, abs, min, max, clamp, lerp, smoothstep, noise, wiggle(period, amount), pingpong, deg… Ready ones: Breathe, Bob, Spin, Jitter. Saved with the project; a broken formula is refused with what is wrong.
- **Viseme editor**: the mouth shapes keyed from a voice clip show as chips under its waveform on the timeline — click one to choose another shape (or remove it, or go back to what was heard), drag it to move it. Hand-set shapes are kept whenever the mouth is keyed again (dialogue.fix, dialogue.clear_fixes).
- **Help ▸ Examples ▸ Lip sync ▸ A mouth that bends**: the bartender's mouth as one drawing (opening, teeth, tongue) made into morphs by Make Mouth Shapes; his line blends the shapes, with a smile over them.
- **Moods on a bending mouth**: Make Mouth Shapes also makes smile and frown morphs (the corners up or down); audio tags in a line — [laughs], [happy], [warmly], [angry], [sad], [sighs]… — turn them on over the mouth shapes for the line, easing in and out, so the shapes and the mood combine like blend shapes.
- Swapped mouth drawings also lead their sound by a frame now.
- **Mouths that bend, not swap**: Animation ▸ Sound & Lip Sync ▸ Make Mouth Shapes (Morphs) turns a drawn mouth — a layer, or a group of lips, teeth and tongue — into morphs for every mouth shape (rest, MBP, AI, E, O, U, WQ, FV, L, etc), closed, open, wide or round, ready to shape further by hand. Lip-sync then keys their weights: each shape rises a frame before its sound and blends into the next (coarticulation), silence comes back to rest. Morphs now move curve handles too, so a closing mouth flattens its curves.
- **Lip-sync from the sound alone, offline**: Rhubarb Lip Sync (MIT) now comes with ToonFlick (tools/rhubarb) — Lip-Sync a Clip…, From the sound: no text, no key, any language; the text, if given, guides it.
- **Help ▸ Examples ▸ Lip sync**: the three ways on one scene — the pub's bartender and the hotel's bellhop chatting at the bar, both with ten mouth drawings: from the sound (offline), with the text, and two speakers recognised in one recording.
- **Lip-sync a recorded voice**: Animation ▸ Sound & Lip Sync ▸ Import Voice Clip… puts a recording on the sound row at the playhead; Lip-Sync a Clip… moves the mouths from it — with the text said in it matched letter by letter (ElevenLabs forced alignment; a key without that permission falls back to recognition), or recognised with each speaker on their own mouth (ElevenLabs Scribe v2). Also lipsync.clip, sound.import_clip, sound.soundtrack_to_clip.
- **Volume as on a player**: the speaker by the player mutes and unmutes (muting mid-play goes quiet at once), the slider beside it sets the volume, the wheel over them turns it up or down; remembered for next time.
- **Dialogue with ElevenLabs (Eleven v4)** — Animation ▸ Sound & Lip Sync ▸ Dialogue…: write lines for the characters, pick a voice for each from your ElevenLabs account, Generate — the lines are said together into a sound clip at the playhead, and each character's mouth is keyed from when every letter is said (rest, MBP, AI, E, O, U, WQ, FV, L, etc), not just from loudness. Audio tags like [whispers] or [laughs] set the tone. Your own key (Preferences ▸ Services, or ELEVENLABS_API_KEY), kept on your computer; text is sent only when you press Generate. Also as commands: dialogue.generate, dialogue.voices, dialogue.lipsync.
- **Sound clips** next to the soundtrack: dialogue takes sit on the timeline's sound row (names and a bar per line); playback and renders mix them with the music. sound.clip_set moves one (its mouths come along), sets its volume; sound.clip_remove.
- **Move a joystick on the stage**: with Pose Bones, drag its frame (square, ring, slider track) or its name to put it somewhere else, on any frame — its recorded animation comes along; the handle inside still plays the stick.
- **Help ▸ Examples ▸ Production ▸ Luna: a street at night**: an old town corner drawn as an SVG and lit only by light layers — the night, the street lamp with a flare, a stuttering neon sign, the windows, the moon, and the car's headlights driving in with it; the cat watches the car; a little piano waltz.
- **Help ▸ Examples ▸ Production ▸ The Irish pub**: a painted pub with a vector bartender on the bellhop's rig, standing behind the counter — he talks: a welcome and a toast, "have a seat", a guest's order from off screen, said by ElevenLabs voices (the takes ship with the example), his ten new mouth drawings keyed from the dialogue; a flickering fire, lamps and a candle as light layers; a jig on the built-in piano.
- The Effect Guide and Animate Text render previews only for the cards on screen, and keep their first frames when closed, so they open with pictures at once.
- **The examples gallery opens at once**: small pictures of the examples come with the app and only the cards on screen load theirs (before, every example was built and rendered as the gallery opened).
- **Light layers** (Layer ▸ New ▸ Light, or the lamp button in Layers): a light is a layer now — move, turn and animate it like any layer, pick it by its lamp sign on the stage. Type Point, Spotlight (points the way the layer is turned; its cone shows on the stage) or Ambient (the night: the whole picture down towards its colour, the other lights shine through it). Colour, intensity, reach, softness, cone, haze and a lens flare, all keyable in Properties and on the timeline; the layer's opacity dims it.
- **Lens flare on any layer**: on a layer's effects it shines from the layer's pivot (X, Y shift it) — a glowing drawing, a sign, a sparkle.
- Hotel at night now lit by light layers (a flickering lamp, a flare, moonlight as a spotlight).
- **Glow with a threshold (bloom)**: Threshold (and its Softness) on the Glow effect — only what's brighter than it glows, in its own colours. Scene ▸ Effects ▸ Bloom uses it.
- **Lens flare** effect: the flare a lens catches from a bright light at X, Y in the scene — a core, star rays, a streak, a halo and coloured ghosts down the line through the middle of the picture; size, strength, colour, streak and ghosts, all keyable.
- **Light** effect: a round lamp or a spotlight (Cone, Angle) at X, Y that lights what's around it, with Softness, Darken (the rest of the picture down: night scenes with pools of light) and Haze (the light in the air). Lights add up; flicker or pulse them like any effect.
- **Help ▸ Examples ▸ Light & effects ▸ Hotel at night**: the lobby lit by its lamps (one flickering), moonlight through the window, a lens flare and a bloom.
- **File ▸ Import ▸ Photoshop or Layered Picture…**: Photoshop .psd with its layers and folders, as picture layers where they sat on the canvas (saved as PNGs beside the project) — visibility, opacity, layer masks, fill opacity, clipping masks (a layer clipped to the one below) and blend modes (the light modes as Overlay); layer styles (stroke, shadow…) are listed as not brought over. Also OpenRaster .ora (Krita, GIMP, MyPaint) with its layers, and Krita .kra (its merged picture).
- **File ▸ Import ▸ Illustrator, PDF or EPS…**: Adobe Illustrator .ai (saved PDF-compatible, the default) and PDF pages as editable vector shapes — text as outlines, gradients, clipping, pictures — through the SVG import. EPS and old PostScript .ai with Ghostscript installed; without it an EPS's preview picture comes in and the status says what to install.
- Pictures: WebP, BMP, TIFF and TGA besides PNG, JPEG and GIF.
- Clipping and group masks take pictures too (by their alpha), not only filled shapes.
- **Animation ▸ Animate Text…** (and Text animation in Properties): ready-made letter animations for text layers, each letter on its own clock, one after another — Fade, Pop, Drop & bounce, Rise, Slide, Spin, Zoom, Flip, Typewriter, Wobble (letters arrive, or leave with Out), and Wave, Bounce, Jitter, Pulse, Swing (keep moving). Picked in a window of cards with moving previews; start, letters apart, length, order (→ ← ↔ random) and strength set in Properties. The text stays editable — the animation goes with it. Help ▸ Examples ▸ Motion ▸ Text animations shows them all.
- **File ▸ Import ▸ SVG Drawing…** (also Import ▸ Any File, dropping a file on the window, and Edit ▸ Paste Special ▸ Picture from Clipboard for SVG copied from Figma, Inkscape or Illustrator): a drawing comes over as editable vector shapes — paths, fills, lines, linear and radial gradients, holes, text as outlines (colour emoji too) and clipping paths (as an Intersect shape in the clipped part's layer). Named groups (Inkscape labels, Illustrator layer names) become nested layers turning around their `data-pivot-x/y`, ready to animate; editors' automatic "g123" groups merge into their parent. A drawing larger than the scene is scaled down to fit (on the layer). The status bar says what didn't come over (masks, filters, embedded pictures, patterns, dashes).
- **Help ▸ Examples ▸ Characters & rigs ▸ Hotel after midnight (SVG rigged)**: an SVG drawing imported and rigged — the bellhop on the humanoid template's bones (IK feet planted, IK hands riding on the chest, knee and elbow poles, the trousers bound point by point so the knees bend), the cat with a spring tail. ## 0.13.0 — Graph editor & motion paths (2026-10-24)
- **Curves like a studio graph editor**: every key can have a **tangent mode** — Auto (smooth, never past the keys around it), Spline (smooth through the keys), Linear, Flat or Free — with **handles that have a weight** (how far they pull) and can be **broken** (in and out apart) or unified; keys without one keep their interpolation and ease. A channel's **extrapolation** says what happens before its first key and after its last: hold, carry on at the same speed, loop, loop with an offset (walks that go on) or ping-pong. Old projects open unchanged. Commands: `curve.tangent`, `curve.extrapolate`; `anim.graph` reports tangents and extrapolation.
- **Curve tools** (Animation ▸ Curves, the Graph's Tools): **Simplify** (fewer keys, the same motion within a tolerance), **Smooth Keys**, **Bake to Every Frame**, **Euler Filter** (rotations without whole-turn jumps) and **Tween** (a breakdown key between the keys around the playhead, at any fraction — beyond them overshoots). Commands: `curve.simplify`, `curve.smooth`, `curve.bake`, `curve.euler`, `curve.tween`.
- **A new Graph editor**: a **channel tree** grouped by layer, bone or shape, with search, and an eye, solo and lock on every curve (click: pick its keys, double-click: frame it); a **frame ruler** (drag to scrub, markers, the play range and the shot's end shaded) and a **value axis**, zoomed with the wheel (Ctrl: values, Shift: pan) and panned with the middle button or Alt+drag; **F** frames everything, **Shift+F** the selected keys; curves in their axis colours, **dashed where they extrapolate**, with **ghosts** of how they were while you edit; four views — **Values**, **Normalized** (every curve from −1 to 1), **Stacked** (each in its own lane) and **Speed** (how fast a channel moves, to even out motion). **Keys** are picked by click, Shift / Ctrl and box, **moved in time** (whole frames) **and value** (Shift locks an axis), and shown by their tangent mode; **a box round several keys scales** their timing (its sides) or amplitude (top and bottom); the selected keys' **tangent handles** pull slope and weight, Alt breaks them; **Ctrl+click on a curve adds a key**, I keys every curve on the playhead, Delete, Ctrl+A / C / V, right-click: invert values, pick the keys on the playhead; the **numbers below** edit frame, value, slopes and weights exactly (arithmetic and +10 work). The bar has the tangent modes, Step, Break / Unify, **eases** (in, out, both, strong, back, overshoot, bounce, elastic), **extrapolation before and after**, a **tween slider**, the tools (also reverse in time, anticipation, overshoot and settle, bounce), value snapping and **pinning** the graph to its curves. The keys picked in the timeline and in the graph follow each other.
- **Motion paths you edit on the stage** (Animation ▸ Motion Paths ▸ Show): **drag a key** to move it where it should be — a layer or an IK controller moves its Position, a bone turns to reach (parents, IK and constraints are followed); **Alt+drag** moves the key **in time** along the path (let go on the frame that should reach that place); a picked key shows its **handles** — pull them to **bend the path** (both turn together until broken); **Ctrl+click** the path adds a key on that frame; double-click goes to the key's frame; the dots between keys show the spacing. **Orient Along Path** (Animation ▸ Motion Paths) turns a layer or bone to face where it is going. Keys picked on the path, in the graph and in the timeline follow each other. Commands: `path.move_key`, `path.handle`, `path.add_key`, `path.retime_key`, `path.orient`.
- **Example: Graph editor & paths** (Motion): a ball whose bounce is three keys shaped by tangents — level at the top, broken at the floor — and hops on by extrapolation, and a bee on a path bent by a handle, turned along it.

## 0.12.0 — Production pipeline, menus & rigs (2026-10-24)
- **Wiggle and loudness on any number**: any animatable channel — position, angle, scale, opacity, an effect's strength, a bone — can wiggle smoothly (amount, one swing every N frames, detail, seed) or follow the soundtrack's loudness, on top of its keys (Graph tab ▸ click a channel ▸ Motion; `anim.modifier`). It stays in sync when the soundtrack changes.
- **Drivers write effects**: a driver (bone angle, soundtrack, time…) can set an effect's strength, width or other numbers on a layer inside its bone layer.
- **Effect presets**: fire, sparkles and speed lines join rain, snow, sparks and smoke (Layer ▸ Particles); **impact frames** (Scene ▸ Impact Frames) hit the whole picture for a few frames — inverted, black-and-white ink or a white flash.
- `null` in commands now reliably means "remove" (custom eases, a particle ground).
- **Rig templates** (Bone ▸ Templates, `rig.template`): a ready rig with simple stand-in drawings to replace with your own — a **humanoid** (hands and feet on IK controllers with elbow and knee poles, joints that bend one way), a **face** (jaw, brows and head tilt on joysticks), a **360° head** (a dial turning through eight views of a switch layer), **spring hair** (strands that swing behind and settle) and a **soft belly** (a mesh that wobbles behind the bones and keeps its shape). Built from ordinary commands, so everything in them can be edited. Example: File ▸ Examples ▸ Characters & rigs ▸ Rig templates.
- **Performance inspector** (a Performance tab next to the Timeline, `perf.profile`): the stage's live numbers, and one frame measured pass by pass — every layer's evaluation (with what's inside it), its tessellation and triangles, and the GPU time of each kind of render pass (drawing, groups, masks, each effect) where the GPU can time itself. Slowest first, click to select the layer.
- **Render jobs** (Render ▸ Render Job, `bogo-cli job-work`): a shot rendered in chunks of frames by any number of workers — on this machine or others that see the folder. The project is frozen with the job (its sha256 checked), workers lock what they take, a worker that dies leaves its chunk to the others after five minutes, failed chunks are retried, every frame's time is recorded. **Report** (`report.json`): wall time, frames per second, mean and slowest frame, each worker's GPU and share, failed attempts, missing frames, and exactly what was rendered (project fingerprint, build, machine, user, settings). **Farm files** for Deadline and OpenCue run the same worker per chunk (generated; not yet tried on a real farm). Chunks rendered apart match one continuous render pixel for pixel, particles and physics included.
- **Pipeline API** (over MCP, the control port and `bogo-cli cmd`): `batch` runs commands as one transaction — all or nothing, one undo step, the failing step named; `dry_run` tries commands and answers what would change without keeping anything; `events.since` lists what happened (commands, batches, edits made in the panels, hooks); **hooks** run an editor command or start a shell command when something happens (session only — never from a project file).

## 0.11.0 — Physics, particles & interchange (2026-10-23)
- **Shape-keeping physics**: wobbling layers can keep their shape — *stretch* holds neighbouring points (or mesh edges) at the distance the rig gives them, *bend* keeps their curve — so ropes, flags, hair and cloth swing without stretching like rubber. Solved in fixed sub-steps, deterministic, bakeable.
- **Collider bones**: any bone can be a collider (Properties ▸ Control rig ▸ Collider radius): a capsule along it that wobbling parts can't enter — a ball as a short bone, a thin segment with radius 0.
- **Gravity & wind** (Scene ▸ Gravity & wind): one gravity and a wind with a keyable strength, gusts and turbulence that push wobble and follow-through bones; each layer chooses how much it feels them. Example: File ▸ Examples ▸ Motion ▸ Flag in the wind.
- **Particles** (Layer ▸ Particles): any layer can emit particles — its drawing (or picture) is drawn once per particle — born in a point, line, rectangle or circle, with rate and bursts, life, speed, direction and spread, gravity, the scene's wind, drag, spin, facing where they fly, size and fading over their life, bouncing off collider bones and a ground line (or dying there), and a warm-up so snow is everywhere from the first frame. Ready presets: rain, snow, sparks and smoke. Deterministic: stage, export and the command line show the same particles. Example: File ▸ Examples ▸ Light & effects ▸ Campfire in the snow.
- Pictures can fade (particles made of images fade out).
- **Clean lines under squash and stretch**: squashed and stretched drawings keep an even line all round; a layer or group can *keep its line width* whatever it is scaled by (Properties ▸ Lines), so a character keeps the same line weight close up and far away.
- **OpenEXR sequences** (Render ▸ Export ▸ OpenEXR sequence, `render.final` format exr): half-float, linear light, premultiplied alpha — for compositing. Written without extra libraries.
- **Audio turnover** (File ▸ Export ▸ Audio Turnover): the shot's sound from its first to its last frame plus handles, as 24-bit WAV with the source's channels and rate.
- **OpenTimelineIO round-trip** (File ▸ Export / Import ▸ OpenTimelineIO): a folder of shots as clips on an editorial timeline (cut, handles, render, project); trims and extensions made in the edit come back as the shots' in/out frames.

## 0.10.0 — Motion, light & review (2026-10-23)
- **Secondary motion**: a layer inside a bone layer can wobble behind the rig — bellies, cheeks, hair spring and settle after a move (springiness, damping, gravity); paint where it stays pinned (Weight Paint ▸ Wobble pins) or pin it near the bones in one click; bake it so scrubbing, export and the command line show exactly the same motion. A jelly demo shows it off (File ▸ Open Jelly Demo).
- **Follow-through bones** (Bone ▸ Follow-Through, or Properties ▸ Control rig): spring bones swing behind their parent, overshoot and settle back — animate the body and the tail, hair, ears or antennae follow. - **Nested scenes with their own clock**: a reference layer can play its source shifted (offset), faster or slower, repeating a cycle, or held on one frame (Properties ▸ Timing / Cycle / Hold, `reference.time`), so one animated character or prop is reused out of step — a flock, a crowd, a looping walk. ## 0.8.0 — Meshes, studio foundation & look (2026-10-22)
- Deformation meshes (0.8 groundwork): `mesh.create` builds a triangle mesh under a vector layer's closed, filled shapes (constrained Delaunay with refinement, holes, extra vertices at joints; open lines reported); `skin.auto_bind` skins it to the skeleton (up to 4 bones per vertex, normalised, explicit bind pose); fills and outlines ride the mesh without cracks. `mesh.inspect` lists problems with stable codes; rebuilding a mesh keeps its weights. - Weight Paint tool (G): paint / add / subtract / smooth a bone's weights with a heat map and the mesh wireframe; Normalize, Mirror L→R / R→L (left/right bones paired by name), Create/Rebuild mesh and Auto bind in its bar. Commands `skin.paint`, `skin.set_weights`, `skin.mirror`.
- Mesh editing: add a vertex (click in Weight Paint ▸ Edit mesh, or `mesh.add_vertex`), split an edge, remove a vertex (Alt-click) — the mesh stays valid, existing vertices keep their IDs and new ones blend their neighbours' weights. Rebind (`skin.rebind`) takes the skeleton's new setup pose without losing painted weights; its preview says how far the drawing would otherwise be off.
- Rendering: groups, blend modes and effects use about 1–2 ms less GPU time per frame than the first blend-mode build; offscreen buffers a scene no longer needs are released after a few seconds; `app.stats` reports geometry reuse, offscreen buffers and their memory.

Mesh quality profiles (draft / standard / fine) and `skin.copy_weights` from another mesh of the same skeleton.
- Project Doctor (File ▸ Project Doctor…, `bogo-cli validate`, MCP `project.doctor`): missing files, broken rig references, invalid meshes and weights, effect settings, shot ranges and duplicate IDs, each with a severity, a stable code, where it is and a suggested fix. CLI exit code 2 on errors.
- Portable packages: `bogo-cli package` copies a project and its files into a folder with relative paths and a manifest (sizes, SHA-256); `bogo-cli verify` finds missing or damaged files and refuses paths that leave the package. `bogo-cli inspect` prints a JSON summary.
- Shot identity: show, sequence, shot, frame in/out and handles (`project.shot`).
- Drawings on a mesh bend along it: their curves are resampled along the mesh, so straight edges follow a bent joint.
- 360° rigs: view dials (turn a character or a head through front, left, back and right), a setup pose per view for every bone so one animation works from every side, switch layers driven by a dial.
- Smooth turns: a dial with "smooth turn" blends the bone setups between two views and morphs the drawings of neighbouring views when they are built alike (same layers, shapes and points).
- Stage selection follows the usual rules in every tool: a click picks what's on top under the pointer (any layer) and a press on it drags it; Shift adds, Ctrl toggles, Alt takes away from a box; clicking empty space or Esc deselects; what a click would pick is highlighted. Transform Layer selects whole groups (double-click enters a group, Ctrl+click picks the part) and box-selects layers; bone tools pick bones of any character.
- Region selection as in ToonFlick: rectangle, ellipse or lasso (option bar of Select Points and Transform Layer), dashed outline; Shift adds, Ctrl flips what's inside, Alt takes away; Select all / Select none / Delete in the bar.
- Layer outlines: any layer — above all a group — can have an outline around its whole silhouette (colour, width), drawn on the GPU from the composited group, so shapes inside it merge into one outlined figure.
- Blend modes: a layer or group can mix with what's below it as multiply, screen, overlay, add, darken, lighten, colour dodge or colour burn (Properties ▸ Blend, `layer.set blend`), on the GPU with the standard formulas.
- Effect stack: each layer has an ordered list of effects (outline, tint) with keyable settings — add, reorder, duplicate, switch off or remove them in Properties or with `effect.*` commands. Older files' layer outlines load as the first effect.
- Erase layers: a layer can cut itself out of what's below it in its group (build a figure by adding and subtracting parts; the group outline follows the holes).
- Moho import (File → Import Moho…, `bogo-cli moho`, MCP `file.import_moho`): bone, group, switch, vector and image layers with transforms and masking; skeletons with animation, IK targets and limits; vector art with bone-bound points, curvature, handle weights and angles, per-point widths, hidden segments, fills, outlines and named styles; compound shapes with holes; the camera; and Smart Bone actions — point moves become correctives, bone and layer moves become driven channels, drawing switches follow their bone.
- Compound shapes: one shape can hold several outlines (holes and islands, filled even-odd).
- Bones and layers can be driven by other bones' rotations (piecewise, per direction).
- Switches can be picked by a bone's rotation.
- 3D pose offsets in turning rigs: a bone that follows a body's view dial (hands, feet, poles) gets a depth channel, so its pose turns with the character — a hand reaching forward in the left view reaches forward in the right view, on the other side of the root. Dragging it moves it exactly as seen in the current view.
